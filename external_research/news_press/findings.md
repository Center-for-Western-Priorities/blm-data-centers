# News/Press & Federal Register Findings — BLM Public-Land Data Center Projects

Compiled 2026-09-09. Covers Part 1 (news/press search) and Part 2 (Federal Register check) per the research brief. Organized by the numbered project/topic list in the brief. All facts below are attributed to the cited source; nothing here is speculation or inference beyond what the source states. Where a search came back empty, that is reported as a finding in itself.

**Important cross-cutting caveat:** Several news articles describe projects that appear related to our BLM/MLRS case list but are NOT confirmed to be the exact same land parcel/ROW case — in particular, county/private-land zoning applications (e.g., "Monarch Data Center" as covered by the Nevada press, "Tract Sapphire Technology Park") may be adjacent-but-distinct filings from the BLM ROW case of the same or similar name. These distinctions are flagged inline; treat name matches as leads, not confirmed identity, unless the source states otherwise.

## 2. Valar Atomics — Green River and Raceway "Gigasite" SMR + data center (Utah)

Substantial press coverage found, though almost entirely about Valar's reactor technology and its San Rafael Energy Lab test site — not specifically about the Green River Gigasite (10,271-acre) or Raceway Gigasite BLM ROW cases by name.

**Key facts:**
- Valar Atomics' test reactor, the **Ward250**, achieved "full-power fueled criticality" at the Utah San Rafael Energy Lab (USREL) on **June 18, 2026** — described as fulfilling a milestone under Executive Order 14301 ahead of a July 4, 2026 deadline. Source: *Ward250 reactor rides cargo to Utah*, ANS/Nuclear Newswire, 2026-02-18, https://www.ans.org/news/2026-02-18/article-7764/ward250-reactor-rides-cargo-to-utah/ ; and multiple outlets reporting the June 18 criticality date (Deseret News, NucNet, East Idaho News — all 2026-07).
- The Ward250 is designed to scale to a 5 MW design; uses TRISO fuel, helium coolant, and graphite moderators. Source: *US Nuclear Startup Valar Partners With Nvidia On Data Centre That Conserves Water*, NucNet, 2026-07-05, https://www.nucnet.org/news/us-nuclear-startup-valar-partners-with-nvidia-on-data-centre-that-conserves-water-7-5-2026
- **Nvidia partnership:** Valar Atomics and Nvidia announced a collaboration exploring a **30-megawatt, "nearly waterless" AI data center** in Utah, powered by the Ward250 reactor combined with Nvidia's water-efficient cooling technology. Source: *Valar Atomics and Nvidia announce data center collaboration in Utah*, Deseret News, 2026-07-01, https://www.deseret.com/u-s-world/2026/07/01/valar-atomics-nvidia-milestone/ ; also *A waterless AI data center? A big experiment is under way in rural Utah*, East Idaho News, 2026-07, https://www.eastidahonews.com/2026/07/a-waterless-ai-data-center-a-big-experiment-is-under-way-in-rural-utah/
- **Groundbreaking:** Valar Atomics broke ground on its test reactor site at USREL around September 2025. Sources: *Valar Atomics breaks ground at the Utah San Rafael Energy Lab*, Utah Business, 2025-09-17, https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2025/09/17/valar-atomics-breaks-ground-test-reactor-utah-san-rafael-energy-lab-oed/ ; *Valar Atomics breaks ground in Utah*, ANS/Nuclear Newswire, 2025-09-25, https://www.ans.org/news/2025-09-25/article-7408/valar-atomics-breaks-ground-in-utah/
- **Funding:** Valar Atomics "secures $130 million to accelerate advanced nuclear reactor development in Utah." Source: LAFFAZ, https://laffaz.com/valar-atomics-raises-130m-advanced-nuclear-reactor-utah/ (amount not independently corroborated by a second outlet in this search pass — flag as single-source).
- **"Gigasite" model:** Valar describes its business model as clustering "thousands of high-temperature reactors" on shared campuses called "gigasites," designed to operate "more like an industrial park than a traditional nuclear plant," spreading fixed infrastructure costs across many reactor units.
- **DOE program:** Valar was one of 11 companies selected for the U.S. Department of Energy's Nuclear Reactor Pilot Program, which aimed for three companies to achieve reactor criticality by July 4, 2026.
- **Green River city government:** Green River, Utah is reported to still be "in talks" with Valar Atomics about siting a small modular reactor there; separately, Fulcrum Point Holdings and Blue Castle Holdings announced an agreement to revive the existing (unrelated, pre-Valar) Blue Castle nuclear project in Green River using smaller modular reactors. Source: *Green River to gain small modular reactors*, Deseret News, 2026-05-28, https://www.deseret.com/utah/2026/05/28/green-river-utah-nuclear-energy-project/ ; and *Green River nuclear project revived with shift to small modular reactors*, Castle Country Radio (KOAL), 2026-05-28, https://www.castlecountryradio.com/2026/05/28/green-river-nuclear-project-revived-with-shift-to-small-modular-reactors/ . **Note:** this Blue Castle/Fulcrum Point item appears to be a separate, longstanding nuclear proposal, not confirmed to be the same as Valar's BLM Green River Gigasite ROW case (UTUT106784007) — worth distinguishing carefully in any write-up, since the coverage does not explicitly connect Blue Castle to the Valar BLM filing.

## 4. Wyoming Hyperscale White Box LLC — built/operating?

Per news coverage, **Wyoming Hyperscale White Box** is the original name of what is now **Prometheus Hyperscale** (rebranded 2024) — see §3 above. Construction on the Evanston (Aspen Mountain) site began in **2022** on a 58-acre plot. Reported operational timelines are inconsistent across outlets: one source describes the "initial phase" becoming operational Q1 2026, while another (evidently more recent) describes Phase 1 construction as still underway with "initial capacity delivery by late 2027" and phased expansion through 2030. **No single source definitively confirms the facility is fully built and operating today (as of Sept. 2026)** — the press record suggests a still-in-progress, phased construction rather than a completed/operating facility, which is a different read than the "likely already built" inference in PROJECT_SUMMARIES.md (based on the 2024 ROW-case closures). This discrepancy is worth flagging: MLRS shows the two small supporting-infrastructure ROWs (water facility, access road) closed 2024-07-09, but press coverage indicates the main campus buildout was, as of the most recent (2026) reporting, still under active phased construction with completion not expected until 2027–2030.

## 5. Skylar Opportunities / "Horizon Project" — Las Vegas-area data center

**Major finding: Skylar Opportunities LLC is the corporate parent behind the already-approved Townsite Data Center**, which has drawn extensive news coverage (see details below) even though "Horizon Project" itself was not found by that name in press coverage.

- **Corporate structure:** Developer **Townsite Solar 2 LLC** is owned by a joint venture between **TS2 Investments LLC** (an affiliate of Houston-based **Skylar Opportunities LLC**) and renewable energy developer **Arevon Energy, Inc.** Houston-based **Overwatch Capital** joined under a November 2025 teaming agreement to help secure entitlements, power, and water. Source: *Nevada town battles Trump admin over data center*, E&E News/Energywire, by Jason Plautz, published 2026-07-21, https://www.eenews.net/articles/nevada-town-battles-trump-admin-over-data-center/
- A related entity, **Skylar Capital Management LP**, is described as the "joint venture" lead in later coverage (RTO Insider, 2026-09-01, by Scott Streater) and as an investment management firm focused on natural gas/power markets, headquartered in Houston, TX; it launched a "Skylar Electricity Futures ETF" (NYSE Arca: MWHS) on June 29, 2026. A related LEI-registered entity is **Skylar Energy Resources LLC**, also Houston-based.
- **Skylar Opportunities LLC** separately shows up in solar-industry records as the applicant for an unrelated 3,560-acre solar lease in Riverside, California (2023) — confirming it is an active energy-project developer beyond just this one Nevada deal. Source: SolarEnergyZones.com, https://solarenergyzones.com/owners/6513203
- **"Horizon Project" name:** Not found in any press coverage searched. The BLM MLRS case NVNV106774473 bears this name per our own data; no news source used it.

**Townsite Data Center / Townsite Solar 2 controversy — extensive coverage found (all Sept. 2026-current as of write-up):**
- **Capacity dispute:** The original ROW (2023) was for 19 MW solar + 35 MW battery storage. The amended data center proposal is reported at **167 MW** peak demand — "more than all of Boulder City uses even on the hottest of summer days and more than three times [Boulder City's] average consumption," and equivalent to power for ~75,000 homes. (Multiple sources: Nevada Current, 2026-07-15, https://nevadacurrent.com/2026/07/15/boulder-city-council-seeks-to-stop-data-center-on-blm-land/ ; Electrek, 2026-07-29, by Michelle Lewis, https://electrek.co/2026/07/29/trump-feds-solar-review-ai-data-center-public-land-boulder-city-nevada/ ; E&E News, 2026-07-21, by Jason Plautz.)
- **Approval and process:** BLM approved the amended ROW to add the data center on **June 26, 2026**, using a Determination of NEPA Adequacy (DNA) tiered to the original 2023 solar/BESS environmental assessment — i.e., **no new NEPA review or public comment period.** This matches our own MLRS/NEPA record (DOI-BLM-NV-S010-2026-0080-DNA).
- **Boulder City opposition:**
  - City Planning Commission voted **6-1 against** the proposal.
  - Boulder City Council voted **unanimously** on **July 14, 2026** to appeal BLM's approval to the Interior Board of Land Appeals (IBLA) and sought a stay.
  - Mayor **Joe Hardy**: "The BLM approved something in our jurisdiction. We were not contacted." (Nevada Public Radio/KNPR, 2026-08-04, by Yvette Fernandez, https://knpr.org/2026-09-08/in-nevada-a-data-center-planned-on-public-land-is-temporarily-halted — note: article URL date differs from cited publish date; both captured as given by source.)
  - City spokesperson **Lisa LaPlante**: "We've had no conversations, there's been no chance for the community or for the city to provide any input." Also: "We don't have data centers in Boulder City. We don't have any sort of code for data centers." (Multiple sources.)
  - Council member **Steve Walton** called BLM's decision "ridiculous." (E&E News, 2026-07-21.)
  - City raised concerns about lack of zoning code for data centers and impacts on police, fire, roads, and infrastructure; the project would draw from the same electrical substation serving residents.
- **Congressional response:** U.S. Rep. **Dina Titus** (D-Nev.) publicly demanded a transparent BLM review and said she is "proposing legislation." She later said the IBLA stay ruling "for transparency and public accountability" vindicated that position. (E&E News, 2026-07-21; Las Vegas Review-Journal, 2026-09-03.)
- **Environmental/legal challenge and IBLA stay:**
  - **Center for Biological Diversity** and **Sierra Club** challenged the approval before the Interior Board of Land Appeals.
  - On **September 1, 2026** (per RTO Insider, https://www.rtoinsider.com/142050-contested-data-center-project-blm-land-nevada-on-hold/) / reported **September 3, 2026** (per Las Vegas Review-Journal via Boulder City Review, https://bouldercityreview.com/news/judge-halts-construction-of-embattled-boulder-city-data-center-on-public-land-131498/), IBLA Administrative Judge **David Gunter** granted a **stay halting construction**, finding the solar-vs-data-center environmental review inadequate: the two projects are "fundamentally dissimilar in ways that affect the environment and require meaningful analysis."
  - Center for Biological Diversity's **Patrick Donnelly**: "This tremendous victory proves communities have the power to fight back and win." Elsewhere he said the solar-based environmental analysis was "totally irrelevant to a data center."
  - Sierra Club's **Olivia Tanager** called the maneuver "a bait and switch": "If we allow this precedent to stand, the fear is that this could promulgate throughout not just Nevada, but all over the country."
  - Developer response (per Boulder City Review/Las Vegas Review-Journal, 2026-09-03, author Alan Halaly): the company said it "will keep engaging with the BLM, Boulder City and other stakeholders to see the project through" and plans to "conduct a comprehensive environmental review."
- **Water use:** Company states the facility will use a "waterless"/closed-loop, air-cooled cooling system, using recycled effluent (not potable water) roughly 10 months per year, complying with Southern Nevada Water Authority restrictions; when effluent is unavailable, the system requires "20-25% more power." Annual effluent consumption is capped at 650,000 gallons/day. Source: *Skylar Capital Management Planning Townsite Solar 2 Data Center in Boulder City*, NVBEX, by CJ Jorgensen, 2026-03-25, https://nevbex.com/2026/03/25/boulder-city-data-center-townsite-solar-2/ . The company also says it will fund a city water-recycling effort to "clean and return water to Lake Mead" (per Nevada Public Radio, https://www.upr.org/politics/2026-08-04/nevada-data-center-blm-appeal , by Yvette Fernandez, published 2026-08-04).
- **Tribal consultation:** Not mentioned in any article found on this specific project.
- **This is also the subject of the "First Data Center Approved on BLM Land" national storyline** — covered by Sierra Club, Outside magazine, Common Dreams, Land Desk (Jonathan P. Thompson, https://www.landdesk.org/p/the-big-data-center-buildup-comes , 2026-07-24), Electrek, and Center for Western Priorities' own site (westernpriorities.org, 2026-07, "Trump admin swaps out solar project for data center on BLM land") — confirming this Skylar/Townsite project is the single most nationally-covered story in this entire dataset.

## 6. Silver Springs / Dayton cluster, Lyon County, Nevada (~3,000 acres combined)

**However, the broader Silver Springs/Dayton/Mason Valley area is a major, actively-contested data center hotspot in Lyon County**, per extensive local coverage — though largely tied to different, non-BLM (private-land) projects:
- **Tract Sapphire Technology Park:** a **1,063-acre** master-planned campus near Silver Springs, developed by Tract, with a development agreement and zoning secured from Lyon County. Reported capacity: **700 MW** under utility study, scaling potentially to **~1.6 GW**. A 256-acre portion is being developed by **Fleet Data Centers** and is reportedly fully pre-leased. Full site energization targeted for **February 2027**. Source: *Tract acquires 1,000 acres in Nevada for 1.6GW data center park*, DataCenterDynamics (via search snippet, not independently fetched); Baxtel and DataCenterMap listings (via search). **This appears to be a separate, private-land project from the BLM ROW cluster in our dataset** — no source connects Tract to the BLM cases.
- **Monarch Data Center (Mason Valley, near Yerington):** Extensively covered by local/regional press. Developer given as **Copia Power** / **Copia Power DevCo, LLC** (note: our BLM MLRS data lists the applicant for a "Monarch Data Center" ROW case as **Copia GDC, LLC**, 120 acres — the news-covered project is described as **~505 acres**, so this may be a related-but-larger combined footprint spanning both BLM and private/county land, or a naming coincidence; not confirmed as identical).
  - Reported specs: **4.6 million square feet**, **1,000 MW** of demand, plus a proposed **500 MW natural gas backup plant** and battery storage.
  - **Tribal opposition:** The **Walker River Paiute Tribe** and the **Yerington Paiute Tribe** both passed **formal resolutions opposing the project** — the clearest tribal-consultation-related finding in this entire research pass. Sources: *A massive data center in rural Nevada is one step closer to being built*, The Nevada Independent, https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/a-massive-data-center-in-rural-nevada-is-one-step-closer-to-being-built ; *Nevada county advances 4.6 million-square-foot data center despite public backlash*, The Cool Down, by Kim LaCapria, 2026-08-13, https://www.thecooldown.com/green-tech/monarch-data-center-lyon-county-nevada-zoning-change/
  - **Water use dispute:** Copia's own application estimated water consumption "under 100 acre-feet annually," while Lyon County staff's report cited "under 800 acre-feet annually" — an 8x discrepancy flagged by County Commissioner Robert Jacobson. Groundwater basin is described by opponents as "over-appropriated."
  - Lyon County Commissioners: **Dave Hockaday** (dissenting vote, called a proposed 150-ft setback "absolutely unacceptable"), **Scott Kellar** (questioned gas-plant operating frequency), **John Cassinelli** (abstained, conflict of interest). Sierra Club's **Cullen McGinnis** criticized vagueness in cooling-system temperature thresholds in the county's draft ordinance.
  - Vote date: **August 7, 2026** (Lyon County board). Published Aug 10, 2026 (Carson Now, by Piper Heath, https://www.carsonnow.org/07/10/2026/lyon-county-leaders-face-public-pushback-amid-surge-in-data-center-and-energy-development — note: this URL/date is actually the separate general-pushback article, see below; the Cool Down piece is dated Aug 13, 2026) and Aug 13, 2026 (Cool Down, by Kim LaCapria).
- **General Lyon County public pushback:** *Lyon County leaders face public pushback amid surge in data center and energy development*, Carson Now, by Kelsey Penrose, published **2026-07-10**, https://www.carsonnow.org/07/10/2026/lyon-county-leaders-face-public-pushback-amid-surge-in-data-center-and-energy-development . Key facts: residents requested a moratorium on approvals (July 2, 2026 board meeting); water-consumption estimate cited: **260.7 million gallons annually** (project not specified — likely Monarch); resident **Doran Ash**: "First let's look at the power grid a single data center uses as much electricity as a small city"; resident **Matthew Winterhawk**: "Please do not bury decisions involving industrial power"; 21-year Silver Springs resident **Karen Nelson** said she and neighbors "were never allowed to vote on this as residents of the Silver Springs area or of Lyon County."
- **County policy response:** Lyon County has been developing a countywide data-center zoning ordinance in response to the surge (*Lyon County developing data center ordinance*, Nevada Appeal, 2026-08-06, https://www.nevadaappeal.com/news/2026/aug/06/lyon-county-developing-data-center-ordinance/ ); the county board voted 5-0 to direct staff to revise the draft ordinance (increased residential setbacks, clarified water/cooling provisions) after the Monarch hearing.
- **Transmission tie-in:** A June 2026 item notes a new 345kV transmission segment was added to the Greenlink West Transmission Project (a BLM-permitted project — see Federal Register section below) specifically so Microsoft can power a data center at Silver Springs — indicating Microsoft is also a player in the area, separate from the BLM ROW applicants in our dataset.
